The Value of Reading

Reading is one of the oldest tools people have for thinking clearly. A book
does not rush the reader. It waits. That patience is exactly what makes reading
different from scrolling, and it is why reading remains useful even in a world
full of faster media.

When you read a long text, your attention has to stay in one place. Attention
is a limited resource, and every notification spends a little of it. A reader
who finishes a difficult chapter has practiced something rare: holding a single
idea in mind long enough to understand it properly.

Writing rewards the same patience. Good writing is usually rewriting. The first
draft exists only to give the second draft something to fix. Writers who accept
this produce clearer work than writers who expect the first attempt to be
final.

Do these habits still matter? They matter more than before. Information is
cheap now, but understanding is not. Anyone can find a fact in seconds; far
fewer people can read carefully, weigh the evidence, and explain what the fact
actually means.

So the advice is simple. Read slowly. Write badly, then fix it. Repeat the
process often enough and both reading and writing stop feeling like work, and
start feeling like thinking.
